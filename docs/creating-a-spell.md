# Creating a Spell (summary)

Original-wording summary of the "Creating a Spell" guidance in the 2014 Dungeon Master's Guide, chapter 9 (Dungeon Master's Workshop), pages 283 to 284. This is not a copy of the book. It was written from recall, not from the book's text.

**Verification status:** Nothing below has been checked against a copy of the DMG. Items marked (UNVERIFIED) are the least certain, especially the damage numbers. Check them against the book before relying on them.

## 1. Start from existing spells

Find spells of similar level and effect in the Player's Handbook and use them as a benchmark. A new spell should feel comparable in power to what a caster already gets at that level. Reusing the phrasing and structure of an existing spell keeps rules consistent.

## 2. Pick the spell level

Level reflects overall power and cost. Decide it by comparing your effect to existing spells, not by guessing. A spell that is better than its peers, or that solves a problem too cleanly, should go up a level or be weakened.

## 3. Set the damage (if any)

The DMG gives a table of damage by spell level to keep damage spells in line. Higher levels deal more.

How to adjust (UNVERIFIED in detail): spells that hit several targets or an area, or that use a saving throw for half damage, should deal less than a single-target spell of the same level. Extra effects such as conditions or forced movement also justify lower damage. Cantrips usually scale with character level instead of slot level.

## 4. Choose the other parts

- **School:** pick the school that matches the effect.
- **Casting time:** most spells use an action. Bonus action and reaction spells should be weaker or have narrow triggers, because they combine with other actions.
- **Range and area:** longer range and larger areas are more powerful, so price them into the level.
- **Components:** verbal, somatic, and material. Expensive or consumed material components are a way to limit powerful effects.
- **Duration:** longer duration raises power. Concentration is the main brake on strong lasting effects.
- **Ritual:** allow it only for effects that are low pressure and worth the extra 10 minutes.

## 5. Choose saves and attack rolls

Pick an attack roll or the saving throw that best fits the fiction, and vary the saves used so one ability score is not always the target. Make sure the spell does not hinge on one save that most monsters rarely fail or always fail.

## 6. Handle conditions and special effects

Use the game's standard conditions (charmed, frightened, restrained, and so on) instead of inventing new ones. Strong conditions should be paid for with a higher level, a saving throw, a repeat save, or a short duration.

## 7. Upcasting

If a higher slot should help, say how, and keep the increase modest and regular, such as extra damage dice or an extra target per slot level.

## 8. Assign classes

Decide which class spell lists get the spell. Match the flavor of each class and avoid giving one class every useful niche.

## 9. Playtest and adjust

Test the spell in play, watch for edge cases and abuse, and be ready to change the level, damage, range, or duration if it overperforms or is ignored.
